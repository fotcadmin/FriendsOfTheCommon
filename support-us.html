Transparent Stewardship

What does your membership support?

Membership Rights & Voice

  • AGM Voting Rights: Members hold formal democratic voting rights to elect Trustees and vote on charitable resolutions.
  • Democratic Governance: Direct stakeholder representation in the ongoing protection of Tunbridge Wells and Rusthall Commons.
  • Campaign Mandate: Your registered membership demonstrates strong town-wide solidarity to sellers, councils, and grant bodies.

What Your Subscription Funds Directly

  • Conservation Tools: High-grade saws, loppers, safety equipment, and materials for regular volunteer work parties.
  • Path & Drainage Restoration: Stone resurfacing on heavily trodden woodland tracks and erosion repair around rock crags.
  • Ecological Surveys: Specialist species recording for rare bats, insects, fungi, and heathland flora.
  • Community Acquisition Campaign: Legal, surveying, and administrative costs to secure the freehold for public trust.

Your Impact

How your gift directly protects the land

Hands-on Conservation

Join Our Volunteer Working Parties

A rewarding way to spend a morning outdoors, meet fellow nature enthusiasts, and care directly for the landscape. No prior experience is required—full guidance and tools are provided.

Habitat Conservation Working Parties

Regular morning sessions clearing invasive scrub, pulling encroaching birch, and restoring rare lowland heathland glades across both commons. Full guidance and tools provided.

Community Litter Clearance

Monthly coordinated sweeps keeping pathways, pond margins, and picnic glades pristine and safe for grazing animals, wild birds, and visiting families.

Charity Committee & Event Roles

Help organise guided nature walks, write newsletter articles, assist with social media and photography, or contribute to charity governance and campaign outreach.

Friends of the Commons Tunbridge Wells & Rusthall

The Friends of Tunbridge Wells and Rusthall Commons is an independent registered charity (No. 1013975) dedicated to fundraising, community ownership, and protecting 256 acres of historic common land.

Working in active partnership with The Commons Conservators, the statutory management body under the 1890 Act.
