The Case for Charitable Ownership

Why private equity ownership presents serious long-term risks

The commercial reality of private equity: Commercial investors and speculative buyers purchase freehold estates to extract monetary yield. While statutory protections like the Commons Act 2006 prohibit open building without ministerial consent, a commercial landlord can attempt to monetise easements, access ways, concessions, parking, and licences.

Any aggressive commercial owner creates the risk of protracted legal disputes with the Conservators, deteriorating maintenance cooperation, and attempts to exploit grey areas of common land law.

The Friends' permanent charitable solution: By placing the freehold into The Friends' charitable stewardship, the community removes all commercial uncertainty forever.

Governance will remain fully transparent, democratic, and dedicated entirely to the preservation of our rare Ardingly sandstone formations, rich heathland biodiversity, and unhindered free public access for generations to come.

Register Your Confidential Financial Pledge

A pledge is a commitment of support should our charitable community purchase proceed. No money is taken now. Pledges are called upon only if a formal acquisition agreement is accepted and contracted.

Confidentiality guarantee: Individual pledges are held in strict confidence by the Trustees to preserve the charity's commercial negotiating position.

Pledge Data Privacy Notice (UK GDPR)

The Friends of Tunbridge Wells and Rusthall Commons (Registered Charity No. 1013975) is committed to protecting your personal information and ensuring complete transparency about how pledge details are stored and used.

1. Data Controller & Purpose Your details (name, email, postcode, optional telephone, and pledge amount) are recorded solely by the charity's Trustees. We use this data to calculate collective community support, establish our financial capacity for purchase negotiations, and verify local backing for grant-matching bodies.
2. Strict Confidentiality Guarantee Individual pledge sums and identities are held in strict confidence by the Trustees. No individual financial information is ever published, sold, or shared with estate agents, vendors, local councils, or commercial third parties. Only anonymised, aggregated totals (e.g. “£350,000 pledged by 500 households”) are shared publicly.
3. No Financial Commitment Now Registering a pledge does not collect bank details or take money. You will only be contacted to transfer funds if and when a formal freehold acquisition contract is agreed and accepted by the Trustees.
4. Your Rights & Data Erasure Under UK GDPR, you have the right to amend your pledge, request a copy of your entry, or ask for your personal details to be permanently erased at any time. To do so, email the Trustees at secretary@friendsofthecommons.co.uk or via our Contact Page. All pledge records will be securely deleted once the freehold campaign concludes.
Essential Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Could a commercial buyer build houses or retail units on the Commons? The statutory protections under the Commons Act 2006
+

Under Section 38 of the Commons Act 2006, works that prevent or impede access to common land, or involve erecting buildings or fences, require formal ministerial consent from the Secretary of State. Such consent is exceptionally rare for established urban common land with active statutory protection.

However, private landlords can create immense friction around easements, utility licences, commercial events, and vehicular rights of way across common borders. Owning the freehold ensures the land is maintained with complete integrity.

Can a new owner restrict public access or charge admission? Rights of air and exercise under the 1890 Tunbridge Wells Act
+

No. Public rights of access on foot for air and exercise are statutory. The byelaws that govern conduct on the Commons are made and enforced by the Commons Conservators, not the freeholder. Free entry is enshrined by law.

What happens if our community bid is not accepted immediately? How your pledge is held and future purchase opportunities
+

Pledges are strictly conditional. If the vendor does not accept our bid or if terms cannot be agreed, no money is collected from any supporter. Pledges will remain on standby should an opportunity arise in the future to purchase the Commons separately on behalf of the community.

How does The Friends charity interact with the Commons Conservators? Charitable support and statutory governance working in tandem
+

The Commons Conservators are the statutory body appointed under the 1890 Act (comprising representatives of the Lord of the Manor, the Town Council, and local ratepayers). The Friends of the Commons is the registered charity (est. 1991) that funds practical conservation equipment, runs volunteer work parties, publishes educational materials, and is mounting this community acquisition bid.
