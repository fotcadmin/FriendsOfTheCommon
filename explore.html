Visitor Essentials

Practical Planning & Guidance

Getting Here & Free Parking Rail connections, bus stops, and designated parking locations
+
  • By Train: Tunbridge Wells Mainline Station is a 5-minute walk (400m) via London Road, with regular direct 50-minute services from London Charing Cross and London Bridge.
  • Fir Tree Road Parking: Free roadside parking bays on Tunbridge Wells Common immediately adjacent to the Higher Cricket Ground (TN4 8AH, max 4 hours).
  • Rusthall Common Parking: Informal on-street parking along Coach Road and Upper Street bordering Toad Rock and the cricket ground. Please park considerately for residents.
  • Town Centre Car Parks: Torrington Multi-Storey (Vale Road, TN1 1DJ) and Pantiles surface car park off Major York's Road (TN4 8SR).
  • Heritage Steam Railway: The Spa Valley Railway operates heritage steam and diesel trains from Tunbridge Wells West Station bordering the Common directly to High Rocks and Groombridge.
Accessibility & Trail Terrain Step-free paths, wheelchairs, buggies, and rocky steps
+
  • Step-Free & Pushchair Routes: Surfaced tarmac and hard-packed gravel avenues along the Lower Common, Queen Victoria Grove, and Church Road provide flat, accessible routes for manual and powered wheelchairs.
  • Happy Valley & Sandstone Steps: The sunken gorge of Happy Valley and trails around Wellington Rocks and Toad Rock feature historic stone staircases (the 101 Steps), exposed tree roots, and steep gradients. Sturdy walking shoes recommended.
  • Resting Points: Traditional wooden benches are situated every 200–300 metres along primary avenues for resting and birdwatching.
Dog Walking Guidelines Off-lead access, ground-nesting bird season, and waste bins
+
  • Year-Round Access: Well-behaved dogs are welcome across all 256 acres of common land under reliable voice control.
  • Ground-Nesting Birds (March to July): Please keep dogs on short leads or under close control across heathland areas to protect nesting skylarks, meadow pipits, and reptiles.
  • Pond Protection: Prevent dogs from swimming in Fir Tree Pond and the Marlpit to protect rare aquatic plants and breeding great crested newts.
  • Waste Disposal: 10+ dedicated red dog waste bins are positioned at all main common entrances. Please bag and bin all waste.
The Commons Code & Statutory Byelaws Preserving ancient geology, wildlife habitats, and public safety
+
  • No Open Fires or Barbecues: Strictly prohibited across all seasons due to severe dry heathland and peat wildfire hazard.
  • Leave No Trace: Please take all litter home to protect wildlife, birds, and grazing areas from plastics and glass.
  • Cycling Regulations: Cycling is restricted to designated surfaced bridleways and perimeter roads to protect pedestrians and fragile turf.
  • Sandstone Preservation: Our Ardingly Sandstone formations are 135 million years old. Scramble with care; never carve into or deface rock faces.

Ancient Habitat Protection • 256 Acres

A Living Sanctuary: Never Landscaped, Never Cultivated

Unlike most public open spaces, Tunbridge Wells and Rusthall Commons have never been landscaped or cultivated. They provide an unusually rich refuge for wildlife within our urban area.

Deliberate planting of ornamental and commemorative trees between 1860 and 1940 led unintentionally to the return of woodland as traditional livestock grazing ceased in the inter-war years. Today, the Commons are a delicate mosaic: surviving heath and acid grassland coexist with secondary woodland, sandstone clearings, and semi-natural ponds like the Marlpit.

Active conservation management keeps this essential balance by containing woodland spread and expanding open heath—and our long-established resident butterflies, dragonflies, bats, and birds are flourishing in consequence.

Friends of the Commons Tunbridge Wells & Rusthall

The Friends of Tunbridge Wells and Rusthall Commons is an independent registered charity (No. 1013975) dedicated to fundraising, community ownership, and protecting 256 acres of historic common land.

Working in active partnership with The Commons Conservators, the statutory management body under the 1890 Act.
