About The Friends & Contact

Born from community resilience after the Great Storm of 1987, working alongside the Commons Conservators to care for our natural heritage.

Our Purpose & Calling

“To protect, enhance, and secure permanent community ownership of Tunbridge Wells and Rusthall Commons — ensuring these 256 acres of ancient sandstone, tranquil woodland, and rare wildlife remain free, open, and cherished for generations to come.”

The Friends of Tunbridge Wells & Rusthall Commons (Registered Charity No. 1013975) is the independent community guardian, membership body, and fundraising arm for our historic commons.

🛡️

1. Secure Community Ownership

Spearheading the historic community bid to buy the Commons freehold out of private hands and vest them in permanent community ownership.

🌿

2. Conserve Habitats & Wildlife

Funding ecological surveys, wetland restoration at Fir Tree Pond, veteran tree stewardship, and hands-on volunteer conservation working parties.

🥾

3. Welcome & Inspire Visitors

Acting as the digital visitor centre — publishing free illustrated trail maps, accessibility guides, family nature sheets, and guided educational walks.

Our Story

Dedicated to the Commons since 1991

The Friends were established in 1991 following the widespread destruction caused across southern England by the Great Storm of October 1987. Hundreds of ancient beech and oak trees across Tunbridge Wells and Rusthall Commons were flattened overnight.

Local residents came together to support clearance, tree replanting, and ensure the commons recovered. Over the past three decades, our charity has evolved into a key community anchor, raising funds for practical projects, tools, and ecological stewardship.

Our Partnership with the Commons Conservators

Visit twcommons.org →

The statutory day-to-day management of the Commons lies with the Commons Conservators, a body constituted by Act of Parliament in 1890 comprising representatives of the Freeholder, Tunbridge Wells Borough Council, and Rusthall Parish Council.

The Friends work closely alongside the Conservators, funding enhancements outside their statutory budget such as specialist surveys, educational materials, trail restoration, and community volunteer working parties.
