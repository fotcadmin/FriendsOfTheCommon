The Threat & Our Philosophy

Keeping the Commons in the hands of the people for generations to come

The freehold of Tunbridge Wells and Rusthall Commons is currently on the open market. For over a century, the 1890 Tunbridge Wells Improvement Act has guaranteed public access, but ownership by speculative private equity or commercial investors poses unprecedented risks of neglect, commercial exploitation, and legal dispute.

Our philosophy is simple: this landscape belongs to everyone. We want people from near and far to walk beneath the towering Wellington Rocks, spot rare heathland wildlife, and feel the freedom that only ancient common land provides.

Our community bid secures this land in perpetual charitable trust. Over 3,000 residents and supporters have already pledged their backing. By acquiring the freehold, The Friends and the Conservators will ensure no commercial interest can ever threaten free access, biodiversity, or the quiet solace of the commons.

Whether you walk your dog here every morning or are discovering these sandstone wonders for the first time, your voice and your support will decide their future.

1. Explore the Commons & Ancient Geology Wellington Rocks, Toad Rock, Happy Valley, and self-guided nature trails
+

Tunbridge Wells and Rusthall Commons feature some of the most striking natural rock formations in southern England. Sculpted over 135 million years from Ardingly sandstone, these immense rock clusters have captured imaginations for centuries.

Principal Landmarks

  • Wellington Rocks: Massive sandstone outcrops on Tunbridge Wells Common, popular with families, climbers, and artists.
  • Toad Rock & The Amphitheatre: A famous natural rock formation resembling a giant toad, located on Rusthall Common.
  • Happy Valley: A steep wooded ravine with ancient stone steps leading through tranquil canopy.
  • Mount Edgcumbe & The Roman Bank: Historic high ground offering panoramic views across the town and High Weald.

All 256 acres are entirely free to explore at all times. Download our free illustrated trail maps or join our monthly guided ecology walks led by local naturalists.

2. The Commons Sale & Why Community Ownership Matters The threat of private equity, the 1890 Act, and the status of our charity bid
+

In 2024, the freehold of Tunbridge Wells and Rusthall Commons was placed on the commercial market. While the 1890 Tunbridge Wells Improvement Act guarantees public access and mandates management by the Commons Conservators, the landowner holds vital property rights and legal standing.

The Risks of Private Equity Acquisition

  • Commercial Exploitation: Pressure to monetise car parks, easements, concessions, and events.
  • Encroachment Disputes: Costly legal battles over boundaries and access ways.
  • Underfunding Stewardship: Failure to co-fund ecological restoration, tree safety, and path maintenance.

The Community Solution: The Friends submitted a credible, fully funded community acquisition proposal. Owning the freehold ensures the land remains in public charitable stewardship forever, protected against speculation.

4. Visiting Information, Parking & Access Transport links, free parking, accessibility, dog code, and regulations
+

The Commons are located in the heart of Royal Tunbridge Wells and Rusthall, accessible easily by foot, rail, or car.

Practical Visitor Essentials

  • Admission: Always free and open 24 hours a day, 365 days a year.
  • By Train: Tunbridge Wells railway station is a 7-minute walk from the edge of the Common (direct 50-minute trains from London Charing Cross).
  • Parking: Free visitor parking bays along Fir Tree Road (TN4 8AH) and Coach & Horses / Rusthall Common bays.
  • Dog Walkers: Welcome throughout. Please keep dogs under close control during ground-nesting bird season (March–July) and bag all waste.
  • Protection Code: No unauthorised motor vehicles, no open fires or disposable barbecues, and please take all litter home.
