Understanding Our Roles

The Commons Conservators and The Friends: What’s the difference?

Two separate organisations working together for the Commons.

Since 1890

The Commons Conservators

Since 1890, Tunbridge Wells Commons Conservators have been the statutory body responsible for the strategic management and protection of Tunbridge Wells and Rusthall Commons. Made up of 12 voluntary members representing Tunbridge Wells Borough Council, the Manor of Rusthall and the Freehold Tenants, the Conservators work to preserve the Commons’ unique landscapes, ecology and heritage, helping to keep them welcoming, accessible and enjoyable for everyone.

Visit twcommons.org →
Independent Charity (No. 1013975)

Friends of the Commons

Established in 1991, following the increased community interest in the Commons after the Great Storm of 1987, we are an independent local charity supporting the long-term protection and enjoyment of Tunbridge Wells and Rusthall Commons. We raise funds for projects on the Commons, organise volunteer sessions and nature-based events, and work alongside the Commons Conservators to care for this much-loved landscape.

Visiting the Commons

Admission

Free and open to explore 365 days a year, with 256 acres of peaceful woodland, sandstone rocks and open glades for you to roam and enjoy.

Location

Tunbridge Wells and Rusthall Commons, Kent. The mainline railway station is just a 7-minute walk from the edge of the Commons.

Access

Waymarked footpaths link the town centre to Wellington Rocks, Toad Rock and Happy Valley.

Facilities

Up to 4 hours of free parking in Fir Tree Road car park (TN4 8AH). Please take litter home.

Explore the Commons & Ancient Geology Wellington Rocks, Toad Rock, Happy Valley, and self-guided nature trails
+

Tunbridge Wells and Rusthall Commons feature some of the most striking natural rock formations in southern England. Sculpted over 135 million years from Ardingly sandstone, these immense rock clusters have captured imaginations for centuries.

Principal Landmarks & Features

  • Wellington Rocks: Iconic sandstone outcrops on Tunbridge Wells Common, popular with climbers and families.
  • Toad Rock: Famous natural curiosity on Rusthall Common sculpted by wind and ice.
  • Happy Valley & 101 Steps: Steep wooded ravine with historic steps through ancient woodland canopy.
  • Mount Edgcumbe: Elevated viewpoint offering panoramic views across the town and High Weald.

All 256 acres are free and open to explore 365 days a year.

Ancient Ardingly Sandstone & Ecology
Wind, water, and periglacial ice carved these dramatic formations during the Pleistocene epoch. The porous rock faces create a unique microclimate supporting rare mosses, lichens, and solitary bee colonies found nowhere else in the district.

The Campaign

Keeping the Commons in the hands of the people for generations to come

The freehold of Tunbridge Wells and Rusthall Commons is currently on the open market. For over a century, the 1890 Tunbridge Wells Improvement Act has guaranteed public access, but ownership still matters. A private freeholder may have different priorities from those of the local community, particularly around access, land use, maintenance and the commercial use of the estate.

Our philosophy is simple: this landscape belongs to everyone. We want people from near and far to walk beneath the towering Wellington Rocks, spot rare heathland wildlife, and feel the freedom that only ancient common land provides.

Why community ownership matters: Community ownership would give the Friends a long-term opportunity to keep the freehold in charitable ownership, with decisions guided by the protection and enjoyment of the Commons rather than commercial interests.

As a charity, the Friends are accountable to our members, trustees and the Charity Commission, with the protection, enhancement and public enjoyment of the Commons at the heart of our work. Over 3,000 residents and supporters have already pledged their backing.
